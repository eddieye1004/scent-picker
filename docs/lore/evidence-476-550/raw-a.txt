Story 

 With an unforgettable scent and a reputation for sensuality, our first major success has become an icon of perfumery. Launched in 1911, it revealed one of Ernest Daltroff’s most burning obsessions: white flowers. For his whole life, our founder was fascinated by these dual flowers, both innocent and untamed, luminous and full of mystery. Narcisse Noir is a memorable confession of those traits.  by Patric Rhys August 09, 2021

“I was thinking about that dame upstairs, and the way she had looked at me, and I wanted to see her again. Close, without that silly staircase between us.”

Fred MacMurray narrates his scene as Walter Neff, rooting around the living room he’s been led to wait in, after first meeting a blonde Barbara Stanwyck as Phyllis Dietrichson - who entered at the top of the stairs, in only a towel, saying in a perfectly sultry noir cadence that she’ll be right down after she gets dressed - in the film noir classic of film noir classics, Double Indemnity.

Film noir didn’t reach its heyday until the 1940s and 50s, rooted from the unsettling German expressionistic cinematography that went strong in its homeland from about 1910-1930. A reach, maybe, but this might yet be another example of the degree hallowed perfumer Ernest Daltroff was ahead of his time.

Narcisse Noir was birthed by Daltroff in 1911 and secured the fame of the house of Caron, despite its dark vulgarity and distinctly unsettling unfolding. Supposedly based around narcissus and an uber-sweet orange blossom, you would think this should be a classic floral perfume, and it is.... but it isn’t.

The fragrance is a bit of a paradox because as sweet as it may be, there is an intense, dark, dryness to it with usage of some very heavy materials in some hefty doses – sandalwood, civet, vetiver and musk. The civet and musk aren’t directly put-your-finger-on-it recognizable but are still unabashedly out in the open for those that don’t turn away.

The orange blossom is tainted. All the flowers have little sun. The sensuality is bracing, black-laced, and not of this age. Some would argue it wasn’t even of the age in 1911. The dark aggressiveness betrays the fact that this is a floral. If film noir had a smell, years before its time, this would likely be it – dim lights, sexy dialogue, sensuality in monochromes, beauty nonetheless, and murderous sweetness.

The founders of Caron, Ernest Daltroff and Félicie Wanpouille

Ernest Daltroff, however, was quite used to discordant, but beautiful harmonies, as it’s been surmised that the name Narcisse Noir (Black Narcissus) was a nod to his ‘muse’ and business partner, Félicie Wanpouille. They ran Caron together, Félicie designing bottles, and taking the helm of artistic direction, while Ernest handled all things perfumed and formulated. They created a home together, worked a famed business together, but were never married – an arrangement that was highly scandalous at the time, but yet they still never hid. Nonetheless, Ernest purportedly, repeatedly proposed to Félicie, but obviously to little avail. He eventually did marry, in 1932, to a Madeleine Briet, while still working closely with Félicie at Caron. You can almost hear a French accent quoting Walter Neff now...

His marriage, unfortunately, was short-lived due to the rise of such severe anti-Semitism (him, being Russian-Jewish descent), that ultimately caused him to flee France for New York in 1940 – leaving Caron, and Félicie, behind. He died only a year after his arrival in Ellis Island, in 1941, leaving Félicie in charge of Caron until 1962, and perfumed by Michel Morsetti, trained by Ernest himself.

Much like his relationships and life, Ernest’s perfumes were far from traditional, more than a little dark, and he was a daring experimentalist. The fragrances under his hand were formulated with loads of muscular absolutes and difficult to work with materials that were, in large part, fairly new at the time. Yet he still saw no reason why the generous use of them should cause him to shy away from layers of bracing synthetic molecules as well.

In addition, Félicie’s art direction and designs were just as inventive and iconic as Ernest’s formulations. The sculpted bas-relief style bottle for Narcisse Noir was copied for decades after. Tabac Blond was made to appeal to men directly, so Félicie’s design resulted in the bottle and package to look like a Rizla cigarette wrapping. The thin, crystal, lozenge-style bottle which borrowed imagery from flapper girl headdresses, as well as Eastern turbans, then all boxed up in Japanese-styled patterns was unlike anything seen at the time, and housed the positively seminal treasure of Nuit De Noël.

As much of a household name as Coty and Guerlain may be, this unconventional couple utterly pioneered Western perfumery and design, with one of the few houses that was solely dedicated to doing exactly that.

Ernest Daltroff brazenly and meticulously manipulated sensory materials and helped to change the idea of what was pleasing, or proper, what a woman was, or what a man could be, filling the atmosphere of the Cotton Club with the stories that he wanted to tell – all the way from France. Félicie Wanpouille had a huge hand in what the perfume shelves still look like to this day.

Mrs. De Laire created many bases, but none so famed as the isobutyl quinoline cored one above, dubbed Mousse de Saxe. In the creation of this, De Laire also created a whole other branch of the firm, dedicated to these very bases, and the ready-made manipulation of novel, difficult, or firm-discovered materials for perfumers to use.

Mousse de Saxe was Ernest Daltroff’s signature base. This appears over and over again in his works, and is the distinguishable darkness and depth that ties his legacy together, his John Hancock flourish of a signature. His genius was able to manipulate it over and over again until it was unrecognizable, yet so familiar. Narcisse Noir, Nuit De Noël, and Tabac Blond, all contain it, just to name a few.

## History

Habanita was launched in 1921 by Molinard, the family perfume house founded in 1849 in Grasse (France) by Hyacinthe Molinard. The composition was signed by Henri Bénard. The release belongs to a moment when the garconne years opened a new social space for women, and Molinard chose to address that space sideways. Habanita appeared first not as a personal fragrance but as a perfume designed to scent cigarettes, sold in small flacons with a glass applicator and in sachets that slipped inside a cigarette pack.

The premise was a discreet accessory for the newly emancipated women who had begun to smoke publicly in the early 1920s. Applied to the paper of a cigarette, Habanita softened the smoke into something warm and ambery, with a leathery facet that read as elegant rather than transgressive. The commercial success was immediate enough that Molinard reissued Habanita as a personal fragrance three years later, in 1924, increasing the aromatic concentration and adding the floral support that the perfume version required. The 1924 flacon was a black glass bottle designed by Lalique, decorated with reliefs of cavorting nymphs, a presentation that became one of the visual signatures of interwar French perfumery.

Habanita built its reception on the same tension that defined its launch. The composition prefigured by four years the modern oriental template that Shalimar would consolidate at Guerlain in 1925, and ran in parallel with Tabac Blond by Caron (1919) in the early formation of the leathery family for women. Habanita found a quieter audience than Shalimar, sustained by Molinard's identity as a Grasse-based family house rather than a Parisian luxury name. The perfume remained a cult reference through the twentieth century, worn by an audience of writers, gallerists and intellectuals rather than a mass market.

The name Habanita evokes the Cuban tobacco of Havana, an indirect reference to the cigars and cigarettes that the perfume was first designed to scent. The Spanish diminutive softens the masculine word habano into a feminine signal, in keeping with the dual coding the product carried from the start. Habanita remains in production, with both the original eau de toilette and a modern eau de parfum released in 2012; the house is still family owned in its fifth generation, with boutiques in Grasse and Paris (France).

# Habanita, A Century Old Legend: Interview With Célia Lerouge Bénard of Molinard 

 

 by Sophie Normand April 16, 2021 

🚨 This article is archived and available in its entirety for free for registered members only. Please login or register to read more.

I had the pleasure of talking with Célia Lerouge Bénard, director of Molinard and 5th generation of this Grasse family, on the occasion of the 100th anniversary of the legendary Habanita perfume. Because this is my favorite perfume (along with Guerlain's L’Heure Bleue), it was well worth my time to unravel some of the mystery of a century of iconic sensuality.

Bonjour Célia! Habanita celebrates its 100th anniversary this year, a fragrance that was first launched in 1921 in the form of scented cigarette sachets. How did it evolve into a perfume for the skin? 

Habanita was born in a post-war context when it was time for freedom and celebration. In full emancipation mode, women cut their hair and shortened their skirts. These famous “flappers” then discovered cigarettes. The Habanita scented sachets were a way of elegantly dressing this new fashion, of feminizing a masculine code that they had appropriated. In addition, the Habanita scent was very new for the time, with its more sensual character, and the first to openly showcase vetiver in female perfume. This daring fragrance, therefore, had to be introduced gently, through several stages; first to scent the tobacco, then as a soap, then as a skin perfume in its own right.

Habanita is the pioneering perfume of Maison Molinard. Did the house have any other notable successes at the time? 

This fragrance has indeed established itself as a benchmark and almost a brand within our brand. There were, of course, other successes, but Habanita remains Molinard's most famous perfume. No doubt because she was a bit of a revolutionary for her time, one of those scents that will not be forgotten.

100 years later, and the name of the perfumer who signed it in 1921 seems to be still unknown? Do you have some anecdotes relating to its creation to share with us? 

Habanita was signed by a certain Monsieur Boucanier, with whom my great-grandfather, a chemist by profession, worked. I don't really have anecdotes to tell since it goes so far back, but from memory, I recall that the formula was, among other things, the result of an error in the dosage or the quality of vetiver, a keynote of Habanita.

Why did you upgrade Habanita (originally an eau de toilette, reputedly the most tenacious perfume in the world) to an Eau de Parfum concentration in 2012, a "reformulation" quite faithful to the original? 

We haven't really reformulated Habanita; we just changed its concentration, without touching the formula, because Habanita is a "capricious" perfume; it is very delicate, and it's hard to touch it without completely upsetting its balance. This is why the current formula is close to the original; it slightly erases the somewhat sour and harsh top notes of the eau de toilette to draw the development quicker to the heart and the base. The changes were in part due to the restrictions relating to certain raw materials.

How do you explain Habanita's continued success a century later? That aura of sensuality and mystery that she still exudes, despite her impressive age? 

Perhaps because its avant-garde character has stood the test of time without losing any of its beauty. It seems this scent of daring, freedom, and women's emancipation is still relevant today. And this is because we live in a period of change and progress, where women continue, even today, to struggle to assert themselves. A phenomenon that is still echoed, 100 years later, by Habanita.

Have you planned any special events, despite the situation of the world, for Habanita's birthday?

Rather than a pale scent variation, which will never equal the beauty of Habanita, a complex fragrance so difficult to handle or re-orchestrate, I chose to dress the bottle in red for the occasion, its second color. And to add a signature to it, a symbol of the 1920s when it was created. This special edition aims to be a tribute to highlight this masterpiece.

Ari army.

Courtesy of YouTube/ Ariana Grande

When it comes to depicting the effects of beauty products, perfume presents a tricky problem. It’s invisible, which means translating it into something you can see requires plenty of imagination. Luckily, it just so happens that Ariana Grande is more than up to the task. The superstar songstress has starred in plenty of imaginative campaigns before, including one memorable video for MAC Cosmetics that involved her transforming into a supersized doll. As much as we enjoyed watching Ari model a larger-than-life ponytail, her latest beauty video might be her best one yet. Technically speaking, it’s an announcement of her upcoming scent, Sweet Like Candy by Ariana Grande — but it completely breaks the mold of your standard scent campaign.

The best way we can think to sum up the quirky clip is that it features clones, cones and candy. Let’s start with the first one: Ari basically cloned herself for the shoot, starring as an ice cream truck driver, a casual café customer, and a baker. We see cups of coffee being sipped, baguettes being waved around, and a quite a few splattered cones and puddles of melted ice cream. One of the Aris eventually ascends the ice cream truck, where she spritzes herself with clouds of sugary scent, triumphant. It’s honestly the best thing you’ll see today, and manages to convey Sweet Like Candy’s smell in a totally novel way.

“Ariana was excited about creating a vibe that was tongue-in-cheek and had some playful, unexpected elements to it,” explains director Hannah Lux Davis. “I wanted to create a scenario where all the flavors come together in an unexpected way. An ice cream truck, a baker's delivery van, and a candy cart, all colliding in the middle of an intersection, ultimately inspiring Ariana to create Sweet Like Candy.” The result: a gourmand concoction housed in a pale pink bottle — topped off with a fluffy atomizer. You can practically see the sugar explosion in your mind’s eye.

“Working with Ariana is always an adventure,” adds Hannah. This video is definitely proof!

How was Ariana involved in the creative process?

She was very involved, from visiting the fragrance house, to sitting down with the packaging designer and attending meetings with the marketing team. Ariana wanted to ensure that her personal voice and touch was constant throughout the brand.

The design of the bottle and accents are the same for both Ari and Sweet like Candy. Why is that?

The bottle design is an expression of Ariana’s love for old Hollywood, combined with a delightfully plush pom-pom charm. The contrast in the design was a nod to the artist’s playful side, immediately connecting with her fan base. Ariana loved the faceted bottle [from Ari] so much that she wanted to re-imagine it for the new brand, coating the entire bottle in matte pink. 

How long did it take to create? 

To launch a fragrance typically takes about a year. It starts with the artist first and foremost and then the ideation process begins. We work closely with Ariana to develop an authentic brand and partner with leaders in the beauty category as it relates to the design, the fragrance, the bottle and ultimately the creative and PR campaign for the brand.

# Al Haramain Perkenalkan Rangkaian Fragrance Terbarunya di Indonesia

 by Dhanny Septianto August 7, 2024

 by Dhanny Septianto August 7, 2024 0 comments

 

Al Haramain, merek parfum terkenal yang telah menjadi pelopor dalam dunia parfum Arab sejak tahun 1970, baru-baru ini memperluas jangkauannya di Indonesia. Bertempat di Indo Beauty Expo 2024, Al Haramain memperkenalkan berbagai lini produk fragrance mereka untuk market di Indonesia, termasuk peluncuran beberapa produk terbaru mereka.

Peluncuran ini dilakukan melalui kemitraan eksklusif dengan PT Auraluxe Indonesia, distributor utama untuk merek parfum niche, mewah, serta kosmetik di Indonesia. Langkah ini merupakan bagian dari strategi Al Haramain untuk memperkuat kehadirannya di pasar Asia Tenggara, sekaligus memberikan pengalaman wewangian yang tak tertandingi kepada para pecinta parfum di Indonesia.

## Hanya Menggunakan Bahan Terbaik

Sejak pertama kali diluncurkan pada musim panas tahun 1970, Al Haramain telah menjelma menjadi salah satu nama besar dalam industri parfum global. Didirikan oleh Kazi Abdul Haque, Al Haramain berasal dari Makkah dan Madinah, dua kota suci yang menjadi inspirasi utama di balik nama dan filosofi merek ini. Kazi Abdul Haque memiliki visi untuk menyebarkan wewangian berkualitas tinggi yang membawa ketenangan dan keindahan budaya Islam ke seluruh dunia.

L’Aventure, salah satu koleksi fragrance Al Haramain yang laris di skala Global.

Al Haramain dikenal karena komitmennya terhadap kualitas. Setiap botol parfum dibuat dari bahan-bahan terbaik yang dipilih secara etis dari seluruh dunia. Para perfumernya dengan hati-hati meramu setiap bahan untuk menghasilkan keseimbangan dan nuansa yang sempurna dalam setiap produk. Filosofi ini terus dijaga hingga kini, dengan lebih dari 1000 wewangian yang telah dihasilkan, menciptakan portofolio yang kaya dan beragam.

## Menggandeng Distributor di Indonesia

Dengan menggandeng PT Auraluxe Indonesia sebagai distributor eksklusif, Al Haramain ingin memastikan bahwa para konsumen di Indonesia mendapatkan akses ke parfum-parfum terbaik yang mereka tawarkan. PT Auraluxe Indonesia dikenal sebagai distributor terkemuka yang membawa berbagai merek parfum dan kosmetik mewah ke pasar Indonesia. Kolaborasi ini merupakan perpaduan sempurna antara warisan budaya wewangian Timur Tengah dan kebutuhan pasar Indonesia akan produk-produk yang berkualitas dan mewah.

Saat ditemui Luxina, Abu Taher Choudhury, Global Sales Executive Al Haramain Perfumes, mengatakan, “Kami ada beberapa produk yang akan diluncurkan di sini, termasuk memperkanlan best-selling produk, seperti Amber Oud Series, L’Aventure Series, Detour Noir yang sangat terkenal. Salah satu fragrance yang akan kami luncurkan di sini adalah Dubai Night, yang merupakan bagian dari koleksi Haramain Amber Oud Series.”

Detour Noir menjadi salah satu fragrance dari Al Haramain yang menjadi favorit dan paling banyak dicari.

Choudhury juga menekankan pentingnya kualitas dalam produk Al Haramain, “Produk kami kebanyakan berasal dari bahan alami dan berkualitas tinggi. Prioritas kami adalah kualitas. Karena dengan kualitas, produk tersebut akan terlihat sendiri keunggulannya. Bahan-bahan wewangian Haramain berasal dari berbagai belahan dunia, dan dua di antaranya berasal dari Indonesia, yaitu oud dan patchouli.”

## Ragam Varian Al Haramain Laris di Skala Global

Al Haramain berkomitmen untuk menjadi pemimpin dunia dalam perfumery yang unggul, selalu mengantisipasi dan memenuhi selera serta preferensi para pelanggan yang cermat. Di Indonesia, mereka berusaha membawa ketenangan melalui medium wewangian, selaras dengan misi mereka untuk menyebarkan budaya parfum yang mendalam dan penuh arti.

Nilai-nilai yang dipegang oleh Al Haramain, seperti etika, kualitas, integritas, martabat, dan keberanian, tercermin dalam setiap produk yang mereka luncurkan.

Al Haramain hanya menggunakan bahan terbaik untuk menjaga kualitas dari fragrance-nya.

Fragrance dari Haramain kebanyakan dibuat untuk dapat dipakai oleh pria dan wanita atau unisex. Namun, ada pula beberapa produk yang dibuat spesifik untuk pria dan wanita. Seperti L’Aventure Femme, L’Aventure Gold, L’Aventure Rose, semua dibuat khusus untuk wanita. Sementara seri premium mereka, Haramain Azlan, kebanyakan dibuat khusus untuk pria.

“Amber Oud adalah seri premium kami yang sangat terkenal dan banyak digemari. Kami juga baru-baru ini meluncurkan seri Detour Noir karena permintaan pasar akan fragrance dengan harga yang lebih terjangkau. Namun kami tidak pernah kompromi dengan kualitas. Karena banyak orang yang ingin memiliki Haramain parfum namun dengan harga yang terjangkau. Sekali orang mencobanya, biasanya mereka langsung menyukainya,” ujar Choudhury.

Dubai Night, dari koleksi Amber Oud resmi diluncurkan di Indonesia pada gelaran Indo Beauty Expo 2024.

“Salah satu fragrance favorit saya adalah Dubai Night. Fragrance ini terinspirasi dari suasana malam di Dubai yang penuh ceria, banyak hiburan, musik. Top notes-nya sitrus yang segar, dan base notes dari amber dan patchouli. Fragrance ini membuat malam Anda menjadi lebih spesial, jika Anda memakainya untuk datang ke pesta, atau acara-acara hiburan malam. Membuat Anda seperti magnet bagi orang di sekitar Anda,” tambah Choudhury.

## Hadirkan Pengalaman Belanja Parfum yang Berbeda

Selain menghadirkan produk-produk berkualitas, Al Haramain juga memperhatikan pengalaman pelanggan. Toko-toko mereka, yang dihiasi dengan motif kontemporer Arab, dirancang untuk menyambut pelanggan dari berbagai latar belakang. Di setiap toko, pelanggan bisa mendapatkan pengetahuan lebih mendalam tentang parfum dan bahan-bahan yang digunakan, dibantu oleh para perfumist yang berkomitmen untuk memberikan pelayanan terbaik.

Dengan kolaborasi bersama PT Auraluxe Indonesia, Al Haramain tidak hanya menjual produk tetapi juga memperkenalkan sebuah tradisi dan warisan yang kaya kepada pasar Indonesia. Pelanggan dapat merasakan ketenangan dan kesejahteraan yang dihadirkan oleh setiap botol parfum, menjadikan Al Haramain bukan hanya sekadar merek, tetapi juga sebuah pengalaman yang menyentuh hati dan jiwa.
